01 Research at a glance

PZ-rich solvents lead, by a margin smaller than the uncertainty.

Most of the cost of amine scrubbing is the steam that strips CO2 back out of the solvent. Every mixture was ranked by its running cost against the industry benchmark, 30 wt% MEA, which costs — per tonne of CO2 on this basis (mean of the cement and coal cases).

Why PZ-rich mixtures dominate

Piperazine (PZ) carries two amine groups on a compact ring. Against 30 wt% MEA, the model gives it more CO2 swing per kilogram, less steam per tonne, and much slower oxidation. Small additions of a second amine change these numbers only slightly, which is why the leaders are nearly tied.

Predicted by the model

The savings are predictions, not lab results

  • Every saving, capacity and duty on this site comes from a calibrated equilibrium model and a short-cut process model, not from a pilot plant.
  • Each saving carries a 90 % interval. For the leaders it still includes zero: the expected saving is positive, but not yet proven.
  • Prices, and the corrosion and viscosity cost scales, are partly assumptions with ranges.
Grounded in measurements

What the model is anchored to

  • Published CO2 loading data for MEA, MDEA, PZ, AMP, DETA, DEA and MPA, plus calorimetric heats.
  • Data sets the model never saw: Dugas (2009), Hilliard (2008) and Dash (2011).
  • Published CESAR1 : MEA energy ratios and a measured solvent loss over 400 days of operation.

04 The science: what changed?

The October front-runner did not survive contact with data.

In October the ranking leaned on structure-based estimates for partner amines, and DETA (diethylenetriamine) looked outstanding. Measured CO2 loadings from Chang et al. (2013) showed that hot DETA holds on to far more CO2 than estimated, so much less of it is released in the stripper.

Concentration
Show

An important lesson from this research: computational predictions must be validated against experimental measurements.

The same check then corrected DEA and MPA. Their structure-based estimates were also off, and both were recalibrated on measured data before the final ranking.

05 Uncertainty and model validation

Knowing where the model could be wrong.

The model was tested on data it was never fitted to, against published CESAR1 : MEA energy ratios, and against its own assumptions. One question stays open, and it is the most important one for the size of the saving.

Energy benchmark

CESAR1 : MEA reboiler-duty ratio

CESAR1 is an AMP + PZ solvent. The model's ratio against three published values. None of the three is a confirmed pilot measurement: two are simulation or techno-economic studies, and the source excerpt for the third does not say.

Stress test · PZ heat at the stripper

What if calorimetry, not VLE, is right?

Hilliard's calorimeter reads PZ's heat of absorption at 120 °C well above the model's value, which comes from VLE data. The gap grows with PZ's share of the amine nitrogen. Drag the slider to add that extra heat and watch the economics move.

06 Recommended laboratory experiments

Twelve solvents for the first lab round.

The top of each list is a near tie, so the first round covers the leading chemistry rather than many near-identical proportions. Select two or more solvents to compare them side by side.

What to measure next

  1. Regeneration energy of PZ against MEA

    Settles the largest open question, the PZ heat at the stripper, and decides how much of the saving is real.

  2. CO2 loading at 40 and 120 °C for the blue partners

    DEA, MPA and DETA have no loading data above 80 °C, so the stripper end is extrapolated.

  3. DETA corrosion

    Published results conflict: 5–12× MEA in one study, about equal in another. It is carried as a wide range.

  4. Real supplier prices for PZ, AMP, DEA and DIPA

    The cost inputs the ranking is most exposed to. Market indices disagree with each other.
